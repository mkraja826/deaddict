## App name

DeAddict

## Short description

Track multiple addictions, manage urges, and build recovery one day at a time.

## Full description

Recovery is rarely one-size-fits-all. DeAddict gives every habit or addiction its own Recovery Track, goal, daily outcome, and progress history—without blending everything into one misleading score.

### One app, multiple Recovery Tracks

Create separate tracks for the areas you want to change. Choose one as your primary focus while keeping supporting tracks independent. Pause, resume, maintain, or archive a track without erasing its history.

### A daily check-in that respects the full picture

Record mood, stress, energy, and sleep as shared context, then confirm a separate outcome for every active track. Add an optional measurement or peak-urge rating. Private notes remain on your device and are not included in cloud synchronization.

### Rescue tools for difficult moments

Use a guided pause, identify what triggered the urge, choose a replacement action, and record how the urge changed. Essential Rescue and safety features remain available without a paid subscription.

### Goal-aware Insights

Review 7-, 30-, or 90-day progress for the selected Recovery Track. Current goals and older goal versions stay separate. Cross-track patterns are presented as cautious associations—not proof that one addiction caused another and never as a combined recovery score.

### Direct coaching with safety boundaries

Choose a coaching tone that fits you. Direct language never overrides medical-risk safeguards. DeAddict does not provide detoxification or tapering instructions.

### Privacy controls

Use DeAddict locally without creating an account. Optional sign-in enables eligible cross-device synchronization. Turn on biometric/device-credential locking and screenshot protection. Delete local recovery data or request account deletion from the app.

DeAddict is a self-management support tool, not a medical device, emergency service, diagnosis, or treatment provider. If you may be in immediate danger or have severe withdrawal symptoms, contact local emergency services or a qualified medical professional.
